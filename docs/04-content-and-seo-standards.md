# 04 — Content and SEO Standards

**File:** `docs/04-content-and-seo-standards.md`

## Purpose

Set practical standards for useful, accurate, search-focused content that supports SEO, GEO, AEO, worldwide visibility, topical authority, customer engagement, and conversion.

## Page-level keyword and metadata

Each search-target page should have its own:

- Primary target keyword
- Search intent
- Supporting keyword clusters
- Meta title
- Meta description
- H1

Use the target keyword naturally in the meta title, meta description, and H1 when appropriate. Supporting keyword clusters should inform relevant H2s, H3s, and section titles. Write headings for people first; don’t force exact-match phrases where they sound unnatural.

Create unique metadata for every page. Don’t apply one universal meta title or description or assign the same target keyword and search intent to multiple pages without a clear reason.

## Content quality and accuracy

- Write for the intended audience and answer the reason they came to the page.
- Explain clearly what the service does, who it helps, and what the next step is.
- Use only confirmed business facts, service availability, client examples, and claims.
- Keep unconfirmed details marked **TBD** in planning docs; never publish TBD content.
- Don’t imply that a service or audience is currently served if it is only a future opportunity.
- Don’t invent experience, results, certifications, partnerships, testimonials, review counts, or guarantees.
- Don’t publish any information belonging to Okiko High Landscaping.
- Don’t create thin or duplicated pages to target keyword variations, industries, or locations.

## AEO FAQs for every page

Every substantive public page should include an **AEO-focused FAQ section** tailored to that page’s topic and audience. Legal and utility pages, such as the privacy policy and terms of service, are exempt unless FAQs would genuinely help visitors.

FAQs should answer real questions that a prospective customer might ask before choosing, using, or contacting Okiko High. Develop questions from the page’s target keyword, search intent, supporting keyword clusters, customer conversations, and genuine points of uncertainty.

### FAQ question and answer format

- Write each question as a natural, specific question a person might ask.
- Keep each question focused on one issue.
- Put the direct answer first, then add brief context or necessary qualifications.
- Make answers independently understandable; avoid answers such as “Yes, we do” without naming the subject.
- Use plain language and concrete details.
- Include relevant terms naturally without repeating keywords unnaturally.
- State limitations, eligibility, or availability when relevant.
- Link to a related service or contact page when it helps the reader take the next step.
- Do not invent answers to fill a section. If the business cannot verify an answer, leave that FAQ out or flag it for owner confirmation.

### FAQ coverage by page type

- **Home:** Questions about what Okiko High does, who it serves, how to get started, and worldwide availability—only where confirmed.
- **Services hub:** Questions about the service categories, how to choose a service, and how services can work together.
- **Individual service pages:** Questions about that service’s scope, process, fit, setup, integrations, and expected next steps, using confirmed details.
- **Audience pages:** Questions specific to that audience’s needs and workflows; don’t reuse a generic FAQ set across industries.
- **About:** Questions about the agency’s approach, mission, and way of working, based on verified facts.
- **Contact:** Questions about how to inquire, what information to provide, response expectations, and booking, once confirmed.
- **Case studies and resources:** Add FAQs only when they answer useful questions related to the specific case study or article.

There is no fixed FAQ count. Include enough to address meaningful questions without padding the page. As a starting point, aim for **three to six distinct FAQs** on substantive pages, then adjust to the topic and available verified information.

### FAQ markup

- Keep every marked-up question and answer visible to users on the page.
- Ensure structured data exactly matches the displayed FAQ content.
- Use FAQ structured data only when appropriate under current search engine guidelines. Markup does not guarantee a rich result or increased visibility.
- Don’t add hidden FAQs, repetitive FAQ blocks, or FAQ schema solely to pursue a search feature.

## SEO, GEO, and answer-focused content

- Structure pages with one clear H1 and a logical H2/H3 hierarchy.
- Use supporting terms and related concepts naturally in headings and body copy.
- Make important answers direct and easy to find.
- Use concise definitions, steps, comparison points, and FAQs when they genuinely help the reader.
- Add context that helps search engines and answer systems understand the agency, its services, audiences, and page relationships.
- Use descriptive internal links that explain what the destination page covers.
- Add useful alt text to informative images; use empty alt text for decorative images.
- Keep claims globally accurate. Don’t add country or city references solely to capture search traffic.
- Treat rich results, featured snippets, People Also Ask visibility, knowledge panels, and LLM citations as goals, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Audience and service pages

A separate service or audience page should have a distinct purpose and enough original information to help visitors make a decision. Consider including:

- Service or audience-specific needs and questions
- Applicable Okiko High services that are confirmed as currently offered
- Relevant workflow or process explanations
- Practical examples or approved proof, when available
- Page-specific FAQs
- A clear, relevant call to action

If a proposed page would mostly repeat another page with minor wording changes, cover the topic within the more comprehensive page instead.

## Conversion and engagement

- Give each page a clear next step that matches the visitor’s intent.
- Use a consistent primary CTA once the owner confirms the preferred contact method.
- Explain forms, booking options, and response expectations accurately.
- Avoid pressure tactics, unsupported promises, and vague claims.
- Make confirmed phone, email, form, or booking options easy to use on mobile.

## Page content checklist

Before publishing a substantive page, verify that:

- Its target keyword, intent, URL, and supporting keyword clusters are in the keyword map.
- Its title, description, and H1 are unique and accurate.
- The heading hierarchy matches the page topic.
- Content is useful, distinct, and supported by confirmed facts.
- Audience and service status is represented accurately.
- The page has a tailored FAQ section with direct, verified answers.
- FAQ markup, if used, matches visible FAQ content and applicable guidelines.
- Internal links are relevant and functional.
- The CTA leads to a confirmed contact path.
- Images have appropriate alt text.
- Any other structured data matches the visible page content.
